What is currently weakening the site
1. The opening title is overloaded
Your current headline says:
Technology Delivery Leader | Program Manager | Release Governance | Audit & Compliance | Agile Transformation
It contains five different identities.
“Program Manager” and “Agile Transformation” make you sound broader but less differentiated. “Audit & Compliance” may make you sound like a coordinator rather than a governance leader.
Replace it with:
Technology Governance & Transformation Leader
AI Governance · Technology Risk · Delivery Excellence · Regulatory Readiness
That sounds senior, current and coherent.
2. Your site still gives Scrum Master too much prominence
Your current Travelex title, career timeline, skills, open roles, and delivery descriptions repeatedly reference Scrum Master and Agile ceremonies.
Do not hide that experience, but stop making it central.
At your career stage, Scrum should appear as a delivery capability—not your professional identity.
A director or senior manager is not hired because they can facilitate ceremonies. They are hired because they:
improve execution predictability;
reduce operational risk;
clarify accountability;
establish governance;
prepare organizations for audits;
manage enterprise dependencies;
provide leadership visibility;
enable transformation.
3. There is too much repeated information
The site contains:
an executive summary;
delivery impact;
problems you solve;
detailed experience;
career path;
GRC section;
skills;
open-to-opportunities section;
another “let’s connect” section.
Several sections repeat the same ideas.
This makes the portfolio feel longer rather than stronger.
A senior executive portfolio should communicate:
Who you are.
What problems you solve.
What results you have delivered.
Evidence of your experience.
What you are building now.
How to contact you.
4. Some claims need stronger proof or qualification
The site currently states:
100+ enterprise releases;
$250K+ savings;
multi-million-dollar programs;
zero-defect releases;
35+ frameworks;
six AI agents;
“production AI compliance OS.”
These are powerful claims, but hiring managers may ask how each was measured.
Keep the claims that you can clearly demonstrate. For the rest, use more defensible language such as:
“Coordinated enterprise release portfolios across banking and insurance.”
“Delivered documented savings exceeding $250K through automation and process improvements.”
“Built a working AI-enabled compliance platform supporting control libraries across more than 35 frameworks.”
“Designed six specialist AI-assisted workflows.”
Do not use “production” unless real external users rely on the product in an operational environment. “Working platform” or “deployed product” is safer.
5. Be precise about employer relationships
You currently use:
Travelex Insurance — Zurich / Cover-More Group
and describe experience across “BNP Paribas, Travelex Insurance, Zurich, and Cover-More.”
That could be interpreted as direct employment at Zurich and Cover-More.
A clearer version is:
Travelex Insurance Services, a Cover-More Group company within Zurich Insurance Group
Then say:
“Partnered with stakeholders across Travelex, Cover-More and Zurich.”
This protects credibility while still showing the organizational scale.
